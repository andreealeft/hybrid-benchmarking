# If you want a real screen recording

`docs/install.gif` is drawn rather than recorded: it shows the terminal, the
line being pasted and the output appearing. It is enough for the guide, and it
will never go stale in the way a recording of a particular Mac does.

A recording of the real thing would show two things the drawing cannot: the
browser opening by itself, and the icon appearing on the Desktop. If you want
one, `Cmd-Shift-5` records the screen on macOS. The shot list:

1. **A clean Desktop.** No terminal open, no icon on it. Say what the tool is
   in one sentence, or put that in the caption rather than speaking.
2. **Open Terminal.** `Cmd-Space`, type `terminal`, return. Let the window
   settle: somebody who has never done this needs a moment to see what
   happened.
3. **Paste the line and press return.** Paste rather than type, since that is
   what people will do, and it shows that nothing has to be understood.
4. **Wait.** The minute it takes is the honest part. Cut it if you like, but a
   visible pause with something happening beats a jump cut that leaves people
   wondering whether they missed a step.
5. **The browser opens by itself.** Stay on the front page long enough to read
   the headline, then scroll once to show the figures.
6. **Switch to the Desktop.** The new icon is there. Double-click it, and the
   page comes back in a couple of seconds.
7. **End on the icon**, not on the terminal, since that is what they will use
   from then on.

Keep it under ninety seconds. No sound is needed if the captions carry it.

Recording it against a fresh account is the honest test: `sudo dscl` or a guest
user shows what somebody who has never installed Python actually sees, which is
the case the guide has to survive.
